Jun 2026 – Sep 2026Paris, part-time

Founder, WatchGuard

Smartwatch app that helps people break nail biting. Live on Google Play

Problem
Many people have unconscious repetitive habits like nail biting or hair pulling, and I was one of them. You can’t stop what you don’t notice; by the time you realise, it has already happened.
What I did
Built it for myself first. In a five-minute setup, users show the watch their habit a few times; a small on-device model learns their motion and, within one to two seconds of the habit starting, triggers the response they chose: a vibration, an alert, or blocking a social app for an hour. Everything runs on the watch, so the data stays private. Launched it on Google Play as a freemium subscription and grew it through store optimisation and referrals, with no paid ads.
Result
I managed to break my own nail-biting habit with it. With no paid ads, in the last 180 days: 235 store visitors, 56 installs from Google Play, and $14 in first revenue from 9 paid transactions, from users in 8 countries.
Lesson
Being your own first user makes validation fast and honest.
Jun 2025 – Dec 2025Paris

Co-founder, Soara (social initiative)

AI superconnector that matched international students with alumni who had made the same move.

Problem
I lived this problem. Across five study-abroad moves and about $200K in scholarships, the information I found was fragmented and often biased, and what actually helped was always one conversation with someone who had done it before. International students make decisions worth tens of thousands of euros without that.
What I did
Started Soara with a former PwC consultant as a social initiative, to make those conversations easy to find. I designed the AI agent that reads a student’s goals and worries, finds the right alumnus and sets up the conversation; humans give the advice, AI only makes the match.
Result
In the first month: 20+ one-to-one alumni connections, 200+ student conversations started, and a network of 40+ alumni across 13+ countries. I decided to shut it down in December 2025 because we couldn’t find a way to monetize it.
Lesson
A social mission still needs a business model from day one. And trust came when we stopped trying to sound expert and let alumni lead.

What failing taught me

Three lessons I paid for, so the next team doesn’t have to.

  1. Validate before building

    With my first startup I spent a long time building, pitching and entering contests. People liked the idea, and I even won a BNP Paribas startup contest, but nobody paid. I now talk to buyers first and build second.

  2. Timing is part of the product

    My emotion-AI model worked, but most of the market was still research rather than consumer products. Being able to build something doesn’t make it the right moment to sell it.

  3. Know when to stop, and why

    Soara reached 200+ student conversations in its first month, but we couldn’t find a way to make money from it, so I closed it. A social mission still needs a business model from day one.
